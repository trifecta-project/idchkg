Coffee is a beverage brewed from the roasted seeds of the coffee plant. In
everyday use the word "coffee" refers to the drink, but botanically it names
the shrub of the genus Coffea and the seeds it yields.

The plant is native to the highlands of Ethiopia, where its stimulating
effect was known long before it spread through the Arab world. By the
fifteenth century coffee was cultivated in Yemen, and from the port of Mocha
it entered wider trade.

According to European colonial writers, coffee was a civilising luxury that
refined social life in the coffeehouses of the seventeenth century. This view
celebrated the drink while ignoring how the plantations that supplied it
depended on colonial expansion and forced labour.

Historians today dispute that framing. They argue that coffee's spread is
inseparable from colonialism: Dutch, French, and British empires established
plantations across the tropics, and the wealth of the coffee trade was built
on slavery and coerced labour.

In many producing nations coffee is now a source of national pride and a
pillar of the economy, even as debates continue over fair prices for the
farmers who grow it.
